L3.04

Under the hood: viewports, contrast maths and accessibility

Recipe card70 min
You need
L2.10a (tokens, safe areas, dvh), L2.10c (the short-phone formulas), L2.05 (accessible names in the shell)
You'll produce
A model of the four "viewports" a phone page lives in, the WCAG contrast formula derived and applied to the app's real colours, the compositing maths for translucent surfaces, and the full story of the 11 contrast failures and one keyboard failure that axe found

In two sentences: "The screen" is really several viewports that change as the browser's bars slide and the keyboard opens, and the app's layout units (dvh, env(safe-area-inset-*)) are choices among them. "Readable" is a number: the WCAG contrast ratio, computed from linearised sRGB, which the automated scans enforced and which caught eleven colour pairs the eye had accepted, including one whose background only existed when a dark card scrolled under a translucent bar.

By the end, without notes, you can

  1. Name the layout viewport, the visual viewport and the small, large and dynamic viewport heights, and pick the right unit for a given layout.
  2. Compute a contrast ratio by hand from two hex colours, including through translucency and opacity.
  3. Explain each of the 11 contrast fixes and the scrollable-region fix, and why some only appeared on certain devices.
  4. List what axe checks and what it can't, and design a test that avoids false results during animations.

2

The problem this solves

Two things look fine on the developer's laptop and fail for real people:

  • Size. height: 100vh is taller than the visible screen on most phones; content hides behind the browser's toolbar or the home indicator.
  • Readability. A soft grey that looks elegant on a bright monitor disappears on a phone in sunlight, or for someone with low vision. Nobody notices until someone can't read it.

Both have exact answers: viewport units with defined meanings, and a contrast formula with a pass mark.

3

Concepts

3.1 The viewports, precisely

Name What it is CSS that reads it Changes when
Layout viewport The rectangle the page is laid out in. Width = device-width because of <meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width"> vw, % of the root, media queries Rotation; window resize
Visual viewport The part of the layout viewport currently visible window.visualViewport (JS only) Pinch-zoom; on iOS, the on-screen keyboard
Large viewport (lvh, and vh) Height with the browser's bars retracted 100lvh, 100vh Rotation
Small viewport (svh) Height with the bars expanded 100svh Rotation
Dynamic viewport (dvh) Whichever of the two is current, updated as the bars slide 100dvh Scrolling (bars slide), rotation
  • Why vh is the large one. When mobile Safari introduced collapsing bars, making vh change while scrolling caused layout jumps on every page, so browsers froze vh at the largest value. Correct for "a background that always covers", wrong for "fit in what I can see now".
  • The app's choices: min-height: 100dvh on body and .shell (fill the screen, follow the bars); dvh in the deck formulas (the card and its buttons must fit now); max-height: 86dvh on the sheet. Each has a vh fallback for browsers older than Safari 15.4 (L2.10c).
  • Why not svh for the deck? It would always assume the bars are showing, wasting ~100 px when they've retracted. dvh resizes as they move, which on a page that rarely scrolls (the Study page fits one screen) is barely noticeable.
  • The keyboard. On iOS, opening the keyboard shrinks the visual viewport but not the layout viewport, so dvh doesn't change and fixed elements can end up behind the keyboard. The app avoids fixed elements on input screens: the account form is in normal flow, and the tab bar sits below it. (Chrome on Android has behaved the same way by default since version 108; the viewport meta key interactive-widget=resizes-content opts back into resizing the layout.)

3.2 CSS pixels and device pixels

A CSS pixel is not a screen pixel. An iPhone 13 is 1170 × 2532 device pixels with a device pixel ratio of 3, so CSS sees 390 × 844 (1170÷3 by 2532÷3). All layout numbers in this series are CSS pixels. The ratio matters for images: a 74 CSS-px mascot needs about 74×3=222 device pixels of width to look sharp, which is why /brand/mascot.webp is served larger than it's drawn (L3.05).

3.3 Safe areas, revisited

With viewport-fit=cover, env(safe-area-inset-top|right|bottom|left) give the notch, rounded-corner and home-indicator insets in CSS px (on an iPhone 13 in portrait: top ≈ 47, bottom 34; in landscape: left/right ≈ 47, bottom 21). They're 0px everywhere else, including desktop browsers and Playwright's emulation, which is why the measurements in L2.10c have no insets.

4

The math: contrast

4.1 From hex to relative luminance

WCAG 2 defines contrast from relative luminance L, the perceived brightness of a colour on a 0 (black) to 1 (white) scale.

Step 1, normalise. Split #rrggbb into three bytes and divide by 255: cs​=byte/255.

Step 2, linearise. sRGB values are gamma-encoded (stored so that equal steps look equal), but light adds up linearly. Undo the encoding:

c=⎩⎨⎧​12.92cs​​(1.055cs​+0.055​)2.4​cs​≤0.04045cs​>0.04045​
  • Why two pieces: the sRGB curve is a short straight line near black (to avoid infinite slope) joined to a power curve.

Step 3, weight. The eye is most sensitive to green and least to blue:

L=0.2126R+0.7152G+0.0722B

Step 4, the ratio. With L1​ the lighter and L2​ the darker luminance:

contrast=L2​+0.05L1​+0.05​
  • Why +0.05: it models the ambient light reflecting off the screen (flare), so even pure black isn't "infinitely" dark. The ratio runs from 1:1 (identical) to 21:1 (black on white).

The pass marks (WCAG 2.1 AA): 4.5:1 for normal text; 3:1 for large text (at least 24 px, or 18.66 px bold) and for UI component boundaries and icons.

4.3 Translucency and opacity change the colours you must check

The ratio is computed on what is painted, not on the CSS values.

  • A translucent background (color-mix(… 86%, transparent)) is alpha-composited over whatever is behind it: Cpainted​=αCtop​+(1−α)Cbehind​ (applied per channel, in sRGB as browsers composite).
  • An opacity on text composites the text over its own background the same way: .count at opacity: 0.7 turned --muted into a paler effective colour.

The tab-bar case. axe reported tab labels #6f6359 on a background of #dfdcd8, a colour that appears nowhere in the stylesheet. Solving the compositing equation for what was behind the 86% bar:

Cbehind​​=1−αCpainted​−αCtop​​=0.14(223,220,216)−0.86(255,253,249)​≈(26,17,13)=#1a110d​

That's the espresso hero card (#120f0e, within rounding). On short and narrow screens the hero scrolled under the floating tab bar, darkening it from cream to grey-beige, and the labels dropped to 4.26:1. At 95% opacity over the same hero, the bar paints #f3f1ed, and with the new --tab-ink #574c43 the ratio is 7.39. (Compositing the 86% bar over #120f0e exactly gives #dedcd8 and 4.25; axe's sampled #dfdcd8 is one unit lighter in red, hence its 4.26.)

Lesson in one line: a translucent surface has no single contrast ratio; it has one per thing that can scroll under it. Check the worst case (here, the darkest content).

Predict before reading on: fixes 9 and 10 appeared only after 1–8 were fixed, and fix 11 only in the final full run on all devices. Why?

Answerreveal
  • 9 and 10: they were in the first report all along, hidden by its format. axe groups failures by rule, and the test's summary prints one line per rule: the node count and only the first failing node (${v.nodes.length}× e.g. ${v.nodes[0]…}).   • 11: it depends on layout. Only on devices where the hero was tall enough relative to the viewport to sit under the tab bar at scan time (the 320 px Galaxy and the iPhone 14 Pro Max profiles, scanned for the first time in the full run) did the bar composite over espresso.

6.2 What axe can and can't check

axe-core evaluates the current DOM and computed styles against rules tagged with WCAG criteria. The app's scan uses tags wcag2a, wcag2aa, wcag21a, wcag21aa, excludes the splash, and fails on serious or critical violations.

axe checks well axe can't judge
Contrast of text over solid backgrounds Contrast over images or gradients (reported as "needs review", not a violation)
Missing names, roles, labels Whether a name is meaningful ("button" vs "Flip card")
aria-* validity Whether the reading or focus order makes sense
Scrollable regions without focusable content Whether the focus ring is actually visible (it doesn't render focus)
Document language, landmarks, duplicate ids Whether animations cause problems, or captions are accurate

That's why the series also relies on manual checks: a keyboard-only pass, screenshots in both themes, and Lighthouse's accessibility audit.

Incident ticket · #01

Eleven contrast failures that looked fine

Fixed
  1. Symptom
    The first accessibility run failed on both themes:
    Text6 lines
    Error: light /: serious/critical accessibility issues+ "color-contrast (serious): 1× e.g. .red > span — Element has insufficient color contrast of 3.35   (foreground color: #f5c9cb, background color: #d7262f, font size: 9.6pt (12.8px), font weight: normal).   Expected contrast ratio of 4.5:1"Error: light flashcard back: serious/critical accessibility issues+ "color-contrast (serious): 3× e.g. .fc-back > .fc-hint — … contrast of 3.85 (foreground color: #7a6e63, background color: #120f0e …"
  2. Diagnosis
    The report was turned into a de-duplicated list (grep + sort | uniq -c), giving eight distinct colour pairs (§5, rows 1–8). Three causes stood out: small text on brand red (the red is a mid-tone, so neither white nor pink text reaches 4.5:1 on it); opacity used to "soften" text (rows 1 and 7–8: opacity lowers contrast in a way the token values hide); and --faint tuned by eye on a bright monitor. After fixing those, two more pairs surfaced (rows 9–10), and the full-device run found the tab bar (row 11).
  3. Root cause
    Colours were chosen visually, in one theme at a time, at one screen size, and translucency and opacity made the painted colours differ from the token values.
  4. Fix
    Diff12 lines
      --red-fill: #c01f29;            /* light: red surfaces that carry white text */  --red-fill: #c4222b;            /* dark */  --faint: #9c8f82;  → +  --faint: #75685c;     /* light */  --faint: #786c61;  → +  --faint: #9b8e80;     /* dark */  --bad: #d7262f;    → +  --bad: #b81f28;       /* light */  --good: #1f8048;   → +  --good: #186b3b;      /* light */  --tab-ink: #574c43;   /* light */   +  --tab-ink: #b8ab9c;   /* dark */.btn.red, .quick a.red { background: var(--red-fill); … }.quick a span { opacity: 0.75; }   → +.quick a span { color: var(--muted); }  +.quick a.red span { color: rgba(255,255,255,.92); }.chip .count { opacity: 0.7; }     → +.chip .count { font-weight: 800; }.fc-back .fc-hint { color: #7a6e63; } → +{ color: #a8998a; }.tabbar { background: color-mix(in srgb, var(--surface) 86%, transparent); } → +95%
  5. Lesson
    Pick colours by ratio, not by eye: compute them, in both themes, against the darkest thing that can be behind a translucent surface. Never use opacity to make text secondary; use a token that passes. And run the scans on every device size, because layout decides what's behind what.
Incident ticket · #02

The card's text couldn't be scrolled from the keyboard

Fixed
  1. Symptom
    scrollable-region-focusable (serious): 1× e.g. .fc-back > .fc-scroll — Element should have focusable content.
  2. Diagnosis
    Long questions overflow the card, so .fc-scroll scrolls. A mouse or finger can scroll it; a keyboard user can only scroll what has focus, and the region contained no focusable elements.
  3. Root cause
    Scrollable containers aren't reliably focusable by default (some recent browsers make them keyboard-scrollable automatically; others don't, and axe checks for the portable fix).
  4. Fix
    (in Study.tsx): make the visible face's region focusable, and the hidden one not.
    Diff5 lines
    <div className="fc-scroll"><div className="fc-scroll" tabIndex={flipped ? -1 : 0} aria-label="Question and options">…<div className="fc-scroll"><div className="fc-scroll" tabIndex={flipped ? 0 : -1} aria-label="Answer">
    With tabIndex={0} on both, Tab would land on an invisible face; the toggle keeps focus on what you can see.
  5. Lesson
    Anything that scrolls must be reachable by Tab; anything hidden must not be.

8.3 Known failure mode · Scanning mid-animation

Pages fade and slide in (opacity 0 → 1 over 0.26 s). If axe runs during the fade, it measures text at partial opacity, which is a lighter painted colour, and can report contrast failures that no user sees once the page settles (or miss real ones on elements still off-screen). The scans therefore wait (700 ms after navigation, longer after the flip and on results) and exclude the splash. The general fix, used across the UI tests, is to wait for a state rather than a time when one exists (expect(...).toHaveCount(0) for the splash; L4.04).

Q1
Compute the contrast of white #ffffff on #ef3a42 given L(#ef3a42)≈0.218.
Did you have it?
0.218+0.051+0.05​=0.2681.05​≈3.92: fails for normal text, passes 3:1 for large text and icons. (This was row 3.)
Q2
A heading is 20 px and bold. Which threshold applies?
Did you have it?
3:1. Bold text counts as large from 18.66 px (14 pt). Regular weight needs 24 px (18 pt).
Q3
Predict: you set .tabbar back to 86% and scroll the Home page so the cream stat tiles are behind it. Does axe fail?
Did you have it?
Probably not: cream behind cream composites to nearly --surface, and the labels pass. It fails only when dark content is behind the bar. That's exactly why this bug hid until the device and scroll position lined up.
Q4
Why is opacity: 0.7 on text worse than choosing a lighter colour with the same look?
Did you have it?
Visually it's the same painted colour; the problem is maintenance. The token value no longer tells you the painted colour, so a contrast check on tokens passes while the screen fails. A real colour token keeps the ratio computable and testable.
Q5
On iOS the keyboard opens over the account form. Does 100dvh shrink?
Did you have it?
No. iOS shrinks the visual viewport, not the layout viewport, so viewport units stay the same. Use window.visualViewport in JS if a layout must react to the keyboard.
Q6
An axe scan of the results page passes in 0.2 s after navigation and fails after 2 s. Which is more trustworthy, and why might they differ?
Did you have it?
The 2 s scan. At 0.2 s elements are mid-fade (partial opacity, so measured colours are wrong) and the count-up and mascot haven't appeared yet. The settled page is what users read.

Key insightreveal
Contrast falls as the painted background approaches the text's luminance, so the worst case is the background that pulls the composite towards the label colour: the darkest content in light mode, the lightest in dark mode. Binary-search α between 0.8 and 1.

Flashcards

  4. Contrast ratio formula?
    Ldark​+0.05Llight​+0.05​
  5. AA thresholds?
    4.5:1 normal text; 3:1 large text (≥ 24 px, or ≥ 18.66 px bold) and UI components.

  7. Why did the tab labels fail only on some devices?
    The 86% bar was over the dark hero there; the composite background dropped the ratio to 4.26.
  8. Why not soften text with opacity?
    The painted colour differs from the token, so token checks lie; use a passing colour token.
  9. What does scrollable-region-focusable require?
    A scrolling region must be keyboard-reachable (focusable itself or containing focusable content).

    So Tab reaches the visible face's scroll area and never the hidden one.

    Whether names are meaningful; whether focus is visible; logical order; contrast over images.
  12. Why wait before an axe scan?
    Mid-fade elements are partly transparent, so measured colours are wrong.
