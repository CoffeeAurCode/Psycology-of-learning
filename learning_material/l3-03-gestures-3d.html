L3.03

Under the hood: gestures, springs, 3D and scrolling

Recipe card75 min
You need
L2.08a (the SwipeCard), L2.05 (page transitions), L2.06a (sheets, pills), L2.10a–b (touch-action, overflow: clip, perspective)
You'll produce
A frame-level account of how a finger becomes a swipe decision, the physics of every spring in the app with numbers, the projection maths of the 3D flip, the lifecycle of exiting elements, and the rules of scroll containers

In two sentences: The swipe deck feels physical because three systems cooperate: the browser's pointer events (gated by touch-action), Motion's pan session (which estimates velocity from the last 100 ms of movement and starts a drag only on the next animation frame), and damped springs whose overshoot and settling time follow from two numbers, stiffness and damping. This lesson opens each system, computes what they do with the app's real parameters, and turns their edge cases (a drag that never starts, a flip that could flicker, an exiting card with stale props, a scroll container that eats gestures) into predictions you can check.

By the end, without notes, you can

  1. Trace a swipe from pointerdown to onDragEnd, compute its offset and Motion's velocity estimate, and predict the decision.
  2. Compute the damping ratio, overshoot and settling time of any spring from its stiffness and damping, and find the damping below which the flip would flicker.
  3. Compute how much a 3D-rotated card is magnified at its near edge for a given perspective, and explain the angle-driven face swap.
  4. Describe AnimatePresence's lifecycle for the deck and for pages, including why custom exists.
  5. Explain scroll containers, scroll chaining, overscroll-behavior, touch-action and passive listeners, and diagnose a gesture conflict from its symptom.

2

The problem this solves

Level 2 used Motion as a black box: drag="x", useSpring, AnimatePresence. Black boxes are fine until they misbehave: a test swipe that works in Chrome and does nothing in WebKit, a flip that might flicker if someone "tunes" a number, an exiting card flying the wrong way, a scroll area that swallows swipes. Each has a mechanism, and the mechanism predicts the fix.

3

Concepts

3.1 From finger to pointer events

Browsers unify mouse, touch and pen as pointer events: pointerdown, pointermove, pointerup, pointercancel. Before delivering a touch's moves to the page, the browser decides whether it will use the gesture (to scroll, zoom, or navigate back). touch-action is the page's answer:

touch-action Browser handles Page receives as pointer moves
auto (default) all panning and zooming only what the browser doesn't use; a horizontal swipe may become a scroll or a back gesture, and the page gets pointercancel
pan-y vertical panning only horizontal movement
none nothing everything (the page can't be scrolled by touching this element)

The card uses pan-y: you can still scroll the page by dragging vertically on the card, and horizontal drags are the swipe. The page-level backstop is overscroll-behavior-x: none on html, which stops Chrome's "swipe to go back" history gesture (L2.08a, incident 1).

3.2 Inside Motion's pan session

From Motion 12.43's source (PanSession in framer-motion):

  1. pointerdown creates a session with a history of {x, y, timestamp} points, starting with the down point.
  2. Each pointermove appends a point and schedules updatePoint on the next animation frame (frame.update(this.updatePoint, true)).
  3. updatePoint computes the offset from the first point. Only when the distance reaches 3 px (distanceThreshold = 3) does it set startEvent and call onStart: the drag has begun. From then on, every frame moves the x motion value.
  4. pointerup computes info = { point, offset, delta, velocity }. onEnd (our onDragEnd) is called only if startEvent is set.

Velocity is not the speed of the last movement. It's displacement over a ~100 ms window:

v=tlast​−tref​xlast​−xref​​

where the reference point is the most recent point more than 100 ms older than the last one (or the first point, if the gesture is shorter). One refinement: if the reference would be the pointer-down point, the gesture has more than two points, and that point is more than 200 ms old (you held, then flicked), Motion uses the second point instead, so a long hold doesn't dilute a fast flick.

Consequence 1: a drag needs a frame. If all moves and the pointerup arrive before the next animation frame, updatePoint never runs, startEvent stays null, and onDragEnd never fires. A human can't do that; a test that fires 12 mouse moves and a mouse-up in the same millisecond can. That was the WebKit test failure of L4.04.

Consequence 2: velocity forgives short, fast gestures. A 62 px flick fails the distance test but passes the velocity test (§5.1).

3.3 dragElastic and dragSnapToOrigin

  • dragSnapToOrigin: when the drag ends without a decision, the card springs back to x = 0 using the component's transition (stiffness 380, damping 32).
  • dragElastic={0.9} applies resistance beyond drag constraints: past a limit, the card moves elastic × overshoot (Motion's mixNumber(min, point, elastic)). The card has no explicit constraints; with dragSnapToOrigin, Motion treats the origin as the constraint, so 0.9 means the card follows the finger at 90%: a slight "heaviness" without feeling stuck.

3.4 Springs: two numbers, one personality

A Motion spring is a damped harmonic oscillator. With displacement x from the target, stiffness k, damping c and mass m (Motion's default m=1):

mx¨+cx˙+kx=0
  • Why this equation: the spring pulls back proportionally to displacement (−kx), friction resists proportionally to speed (−cx˙), and Newton's second law sets their sum equal to mx¨.

Two derived numbers describe its behaviour:

ω0​=mk​​(natural frequency, rad/s),ζ=2km​c​(damping ratio)
  • ζ<1: underdamped, it overshoots and oscillates while settling.
  • ζ=1: critically damped, the fastest approach with no overshoot.
  • ζ>1: overdamped, slow, no overshoot.

For an underdamped spring released from rest, the first overshoot as a fraction of the distance travelled is

overshoot=exp(1−ζ2​−ζπ​)
  • Why: the solution is x(t)=x0​e−ζω0​t(cosωd​t+1−ζ2​ζ​sinωd​t) with ωd​=ω0​1−ζ2​; the first extremum is at tp​=π/ωd​, and plugging it in leaves only the exponential.

and the time to settle within about 2% is

ts​≈ζω0​4​
  • Why: the oscillation's envelope is e−ζω0​t, and e−4≈0.018.

This is exactly the solver Motion runs: its source computes dampingRatio = damping / (2 * Math.sqrt(stiffness * mass)) and angularFreq = undampedFreq * Math.sqrt(1 - dampingRatio²), then evaluates the closed-form solution each frame.

4.2 Will the flip ever flicker?

The front face shows while the angle is below 90°, the back while it's 90° or more (L2.08a). Flipping from 0° to 180°, the spring overshoots by 1.38%×180°≈ 2.5° (to about 182.5°), then settles. To flip back from 180° to 0°, it undershoots to about −2.5°. Either way the angle never re-crosses 90°, so the visible face changes exactly once.

How low could damping go before it breaks? After the swap, the rule shows the back face, which is correct while the angle stays between 90° and 270° (the back faces you). Past 270°, the front faces you again geometrically, but the rule still shows the back, now pointing away, so you'd see it mirrored (or nothing). The first peak is at 180°(1+overshoot), so trouble starts when the overshoot exceeds 50%. Solve exp(−ζπ/1−ζ2​)=0.5:

1−ζ2​ζπ​=ln2⇒ζ≈0.2155⇒c=2ζk​≈2⋅0.2155⋅260​≈6.95

So with stiffness: 260, any damping below about 7 would swing the card past 270° and show a mirrored answer for a moment. (Below about 3.5, an overshoot above 70.7%, the rebound would even dip under 90° and flash the question.) The chosen 26 is nearly four times the margin. Critical damping for this stiffness is 2260​≈32.2.

damping ζ Overshoot First peak First rebound Display

4.3 Perspective: how big does the near edge get?

With perspective: p on the deck, a point at depth z (towards the viewer is positive) is drawn scaled by

s(z)=p−zp​
  • Why: similar triangles. The eye is at distance p from the screen plane; a point z closer to the eye is at distance p−z, and the projection onto the screen scales by p/(p−z).

A card of width w rotated by θ about its vertical centre line has its edges at z=±2w​sinθ. For a 350 px card:

p θ=30° near / far θ=45° near / far θ=60° near / far
400 px 1.28 / 0.82 1.45 / 0.76 1.61 / 0.73
800 px 1.12 / 0.90 1.18 / 0.87 1.23 / 0.84
1600 px (the app) 1.06 / 0.95 1.08 / 0.93 1.10 / 0.91

At 45°, the app's near edge is drawn 8% bigger and the far edge 7% smaller: enough to read as 3D, not enough to distort text or poke past the deck. At perspective: 400px the near edge would be 45% bigger and spill over the neighbouring UI.

4.4 The swipe's visual mappings

useTransform maps the drag offset x linearly and clamps to the output range:

  • Tilt: x∈[−220,220]↦rotate∈[−14°,14°], so the tilt is x⋅22014​≈0.064° per px.
  • "GOT IT" stamp: x∈[30,120]↦ opacity [0,1]: invisible for small wobbles, fully shown just past the 110 px decision line.
  • "AGAIN" stamp: x∈[−120,−30]↦[1,0], the mirror image.

5.3 What AnimatePresence does when you swipe

  • For 0.36 s two cards exist in the DOM: the exiting one and the entering one. Code (and tests) that query "the card" must pick the right one; the tests use .fc .last() (the newest), and the quiz test bug of L4.05 came from reading the card sliding out.
  • Why custom={dir}: the exiting card was rendered with the props of its last render, when dir described the previous swipe. AnimatePresence custom={dir} passes the current value to the exit variant function, exit: (d) => ({ x: d * 520, … }), so the card flies in the direction you just swiped. Without it, every other card would fly the wrong way.
  • d === 0 (restart, undo) uses a quick fade (0.15 s) instead of a fly-out.
  • Pages use mode="wait": the old page exits (0.26 s, fades and rises 8 px), then the new one enters (0.26 s). A route change therefore takes about 0.52 s before the new page is stable, which is why UI tests wait for the transition before asserting (L4.04).
6

Scrolling, under the hood

6.1 Scroll containers and what they break

An element becomes a scroll container when its overflow is auto, scroll or hidden (yes, hidden too: it can still be scrolled by code, focus, or anchor jumps). clip and visible don't. A scroll container:

  • captures position: sticky: a sticky child sticks within the nearest scroll container, not the viewport. If body became one, the top bar would stop sticking (L2.10a).
  • claims touch gestures inside it (the "swipes swallowed by .fc-scroll" bug), unless touch-action says otherwise.

6.2 Scroll chaining and overscroll-behavior

When a scroll container reaches its end, further scrolling chains to the next scrollable ancestor (the page), and at the page's end the browser may bounce, pull-to-refresh or navigate.

Value Effect at the boundary Used on
auto Chain to the parent; browser effects allowed (default)
contain Don't chain; keep this element's own bounce .fc-scroll (long options don't scroll the page)
none Don't chain; no bounce, no pull-to-refresh, no navigation gesture body; overscroll-behavior-x on html

6.3 Passive listeners

window.addEventListener("scroll", on, { passive: true }) in the top bar promises the browser that the listener won't call preventDefault(). The browser can then scroll on its compositor thread immediately, without waiting for JavaScript. A non-passive touchmove or wheel listener forces the browser to wait for your handler on every frame of scrolling. Chrome treats document-level touchstart/touchmove listeners as passive by default, and ignores preventDefault() in them with a console warning. That's one reason the app blocks gestures with CSS (touch-action, overscroll-behavior) rather than with JavaScript.

Bug journal: failure modes of the stack

Incident ticket · #01

A drag that never starts (mechanism of the WebKit test failure)

Fixed
  1. Symptom
    In Playwright's WebKit projects, page.mouse.move(x + 220, y, { steps: 12 }) followed by mouse.up() left the deck unchanged:
    Text3 lines
    Error: expect(locator).toContainText(expected) failedExpected string: "✓ 1"Received string: "1 / 10✓ 0 · ↻ 0"
  2. Diagnosis
    An event logger showed WebKit did deliver 13 pointermove events and a pointerup. Adding a 100 ms pause before mouse.up() made the swipe work on every engine, and the card's mid-drag transform showed it had moved. So the events arrived, but too fast: steps: 12 dispatches all 12 moves without waiting, so in WebKit the moves and the up all landed before one animation frame.
  3. Root cause
    Motion starts a drag in updatePoint, which runs on the next animation frame (§3.2). pointerup before that frame → startEvent is null → onEnd (our onDragEnd) is skipped.
  4. Fix
    (in the test, not the app: humans can't move 220 px and lift within one frame):
    Diff6 lines
        await page.mouse.move(x + dx, y + 2, { steps: 12 });    for (let i = 1; i <= 12; i++) {      await page.mouse.move(x + (dx * i) / 12, y + 2); // ~200ms, like a real swipe      await page.waitForTimeout(16);    }    await page.mouse.up();
  5. Lesson
    Synthetic input must respect the frame clock of the code under test. When a gesture works by hand and fails in automation, log the raw events first, then compare the timing, not only the event list.
Incident ticket · #02

A flip that would flicker (predicted, never shipped)

Predicted
  1. Symptom
    (if someone "makes the flip bouncier" with damping: 5). At the end of every flip the answer appears, then for a split second shows mirrored, then settles.
  2. Diagnosis
    With ζ=5/(2260​)=0.155, the overshoot is exp(−0.155π/1−0.1552​)≈61%, so the angle peaks at 180°×1.61≈290° before rebounding to about 113°. Between 270° and 290° the front of the card geometrically faces you, but the rule "angle ≥ 90° → back" still shows the back face, which is now pointing away: drawn mirrored (or invisible, where backface-visibility works).
  3. Root cause
    The face rule assumes the angle stays between −90° and 270° (each face is correct within 90° of facing you). A spring overshoot above 50% breaks that assumption; above 70.7% (damping below about 3.5) the rebound would also dip under 90° and flash the question.
  4. Fix
    Keep ζ well above 0.2155 (damping > 7 at stiffness 260). The shipped value (26) has a 3.7× margin. If you want more bounce, bounce the scale, not the angle.
  5. Lesson
    When you derive a display state from an animated value, compute the value's extreme (overshoot) and check the derivation still holds there.
Incident ticket · #03

An exiting element with stale props (known Motion foot-gun)

Fixed
  1. Symptom
    (without custom). Swipe right: the card flies right. Swipe left: the card flies right again. Swipe right: it flies left.
  2. Diagnosis
    The exit animation reads dir from the exiting card's own props, which were frozen at its last render, before setDir ran for this swipe.
  3. Root cause
    A removed child is no longer rendered by its parent, so it never receives new props. AnimatePresence keeps the old element around to animate it out.
  4. Fix
    (what the app does): pass the live value through presence.
    Diff2 lines
     <AnimatePresence initial={false}> <AnimatePresence custom={dir} initial={false}>
    with exit: (d: number) => … as a variant function.
  5. Lesson
    Anything an exit animation depends on must come through AnimatePresence's custom, not through the exiting component's props.

Flashcards

See all 12 cards as a listfor a quick scan
  1. When does Motion actually start a drag?
    On the next animation frame after the pointer has moved ≥ 3 px.
  2. Why can a synthetic swipe do nothing?
    If all moves and the pointerup land before a frame, the drag never starts and onDragEnd isn't called.
  3. How does Motion estimate release velocity?
    Displacement over roughly the last 100 ms of pointer samples.
  4. Damping ratio formula?
    ζ=2km​c​; Motion's mass defaults to 1.
  5. Overshoot formula?
    exp(1−ζ2​−ζπ​).
  6. The flip spring's ζ and overshoot?
    0.806 and 1.4% (≈ 2.5°).
  7. Below what damping would the flip flicker (k = 260)?
    About 7 (ζ≈0.2155, 50% overshoot).
  8. Perspective scale formula?
    s=p−zp​.
  9. Why custom on AnimatePresence?
    Exiting children keep stale props; custom passes the current value to their exit variants.
  10. Which overflow values create a scroll container?
    auto, scroll and hidden (not clip or visible).
  11. overscroll-behavior: contain vs none?
    Both stop chaining; contain keeps the element's own bounce, none removes all boundary effects.
  12. What does a passive listener promise?
    It won't call preventDefault(), so the browser can scroll without waiting for JavaScript.

Glossary

Animation frame
one screen refresh (~16.7 ms at 60 Hz); requestAnimationFrame callbacks run just before it.
Compositor thread
the browser thread that scrolls and composites layers without running JavaScript.
Critical damping
ζ = 1, fastest settle without overshoot.
Damping ratio (ζ)
how quickly oscillation dies out.
Natural frequency (ω₀)
how fast the undamped spring would oscillate.
Pan session
Motion's record of one pointer gesture.
Passive listener
an event listener that promises not to cancel the default action.
Pointer events
the unified mouse/touch/pen input events.
Pointercancel
sent when the browser takes over a gesture.
Projection
mapping 3D points onto the 2D screen.
Scroll chaining
passing leftover scroll to the parent container.
Settling time
how long until the motion stays within a small band of the target.
Stale props
values an unmounting component captured at its last render.
